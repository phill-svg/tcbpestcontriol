[01] The species

Three pests, three different habitats.

Each species targets a different part of the house — wardrobe, pantry or bookshelf. Treatment is calibrated to the species and the substrate, so we don't spray the kitchen cupboards to chase a moth that's living in the guest-room rug.

Clothes Moths

Webbing and case-bearing clothes moths attack wool, silk, fur and cashmere — leaving pinhole damage in stored garments, blankets and heirloom textiles.

Pantry Moths

Indian meal moth and similar species infest flour, cereals, spices and pet food — webbing in cupboards and small grey moths in the kitchen.

Silverfish

Quick, paper-coloured silverfish graze on books, wallpaper paste and starchy binders — usually only noticed once pages or labels show surface damage.

[03] Storage & prevention

Six habits that stop the next infestation.

Once an infestation is under control, day-to-day habits decide whether it returns. Same recommendations we hand over on the back of every written report.

Sealed storage

Move woollens, silks and cashmeres into airtight garment bags or vacuum-seal bags each season.

Cedar & lavender

Cedar blocks and lavender sachets in wardrobes and linen cupboards deter re-entry — replace every twelve months.

Freeze or heat

Susceptible textiles can be frozen for 72 hours or tumble-dried on hot to kill any eggs before they're stored.

Airtight pantry

Flour, cereals, spices, dried fruit, pet food and bird seed go into sealed plastic or glass containers.

Monitor strips

Sticky pheromone or book-moth traps in wardrobes and cupboards catch adults before a small problem becomes a colony.

De-clutter & vacuum

Regular vacuuming of shelves, skirting boards and under furniture removes eggs and helps spot early activity.

[05] FAQ

The questions Canberra households ask first.

[FAQ- 01 ]

How do I tell moths from silverfish?

Each species targets a different part of the house. Clothes moths leave pinhole damage in wool, silk, fur and cashmere. Pantry moths leave webbing in flour, cereals, spices and pet food, with small grey moths in the kitchen. Silverfish show up as etched pages in old books and paper.

[FAQ- 02 ]

Do I need to empty the pantry before you arrive?

For Indian meal moth and similar pantry infestations, yes — we ask you to clear all open dry goods such as flour, cereals, spices and dried fruit. We never blanket-spray food storage; pantry treatment targets the shelves, hinges and corners where larvae shelter.

[FAQ- 03 ]

Is the treatment safe around clothing and food?

We use low-toxicity, odour-controlled product applied by fully licensed, insured technicians. Pets and children can usually return once surfaces dry, and the re-entry time is written on every quote.

[FAQ- 04 ]

Will you fog the whole house?

No. There is no blanket fogging. We confirm the species, treat only the harbourage zones where they are actually breeding, and add pheromone or sticky traps to monitor and break the breeding cycle.

[FAQ- 05 ]

How do I stop them coming back?

Storage habits decide whether an infestation returns. Move woollens, silks and cashmeres into airtight or vacuum-seal bags each season, keep cedar blocks and lavender sachets in wardrobes and replace them every twelve months, freeze susceptible textiles for 72 hours or tumble-dry them hot before storing, and move dry goods into airtight pantry containers.
